ABOUT THIS CONVERSATION

Episode overview

Former U.S. Senator George LeMieux joins Alana K. Vandeveer for a conversation about principled leadership, public service, fiscal discipline, civic dialogue, and Florida’s future.

LeMieux traces his interest in public service to Ronald Reagan and the Iranian hostage crisis, then reflects on building consensus, breaking through government bureaucracy, and listening across political divides.

He also discusses the difficult decision not to pursue another Senate run, the work of the LeMieux Center for Public Policy, his warning about federal debt, and why character must come before political convenience.

Hosted by Alana K. Vandeveer, The Alana Show features real conversations and distinct voices from business, leadership, public service, culture, and community.

EPISODE GUIDE

A closer look at the conversation

These concise editorial notes are grounded in the interview and link directly to the relevant moments in the full conversation.

What first drew George LeMieux to public service?

LeMieux says the Iranian hostage crisis and Ronald Reagan’s inauguration shaped his early belief that public service could solve problems when leaders approached it with a servant’s heart and the tools to act.

Watch this part at 01:13

What does he say effective leadership requires?

Drawing on his work in Florida government, LeMieux describes leadership as disciplined work: setting priorities, building consensus, maintaining relationships, and persisting through institutions that are often designed to resist change.

Watch this part at 04:06

Why does listening across political differences matter?

LeMieux argues that people learn little from circles that only reinforce their existing opinions. He says a constitutional republic depends on citizens listening without abandoning their core values and working toward constructive solutions.

Watch this part at 09:32

What was his hardest political decision?

He identifies the decision not to continue pursuing a return to the U.S. Senate. He describes weighing public-service goals against responsibilities to his family and business, and says the choice opened space for other forms of civic work.

Watch this part at 12:51

What role does the LeMieux Center play?

He presents the Center as a place for students and the public to engage leaders, examine public policy, and practice reasoned dialogue. He also describes the Freidheim Fellows program and the alumni paths that have grown from it.

Watch this part at 18:53

What warning does he give about federal debt?

LeMieux describes federal debt and rising interest costs as the country’s most urgent fiscal challenge. His comments are a policy argument made in the interview, and he calls for spending restraint, closer oversight, and greater accountability for public money.

Watch this part at 22:31

How does he define character in leadership?

He says character means following conscience and doing what is right even when it is politically inconvenient. He illustrates the point with a Senate vote supporting a small-business lending measure despite pressure from his own party.

Watch this part at 27:49
